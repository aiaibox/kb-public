# Tomato ripeness and flavour

**Scope:** When to pick a table tomato, how to store it, and the growing levers
that raise sweetness. Excludes variety selection, pest and disease management,
and processing tomatoes.

## Conclusion

Three rules carry almost all of the flavour outcome:

1. **Vine-ripen.** Picking permanently cuts off sugar input from the plant —
   off-vine ripening only converts trace remaining starch. Sugar gain stops for
   good the moment you pick.
2. **Cut water at colour-break.** Reduce watering **30–50%** once fruit begins to
   colour, and do it *gradually* — abrupt wet–dry swings crack the fruit.
3. **Never refrigerate.** Below **12°C** the enzymes that synthesise tomato aroma
   volatiles are destroyed and the flesh turns mealy **permanently**. Store
   stem-down at 20–25°C.

### When to pick

Six stages: green → colour-break → top-colour → pink → red-ripe → over-ripe.

| Purpose | Pick at |
|---|---|
| Eat now | **8–10/10** — best window is 9–10/10 |
| Hold 1–2 days | 6–7/10 |
| Transport or long storage | **3–5/10** (colour-break) — the earliest worthwhile pick |

At 9–10/10 the fruit is fully in variety colour with **no hard green shoulder**,
a gentle press yields slightly and springs back, and the stem end smells strongly
of tomato. Pick in early morning or evening; twist at the stem abscission joint,
or snip with clean shears leaving a small calyx.

A colour-break fruit held 2–3 days at room temperature recovers **85–90%** of
vine-ripened flavour. That is the ceiling for anything picked early.

### Growing levers for sweetness

| Lever | Target |
|---|---|
| Soil moisture, fruit expansion | 70–80%, then cut 30–50% at colour-break |
| Sun | 8+ h direct daily |
| Temperature | Day 25–28°C, night 13–16°C — a **10–15°C day/night differential** to cut nighttime sugar respiration |
| Feeding after fruit set | **Cut nitrogen**, switch to high-K (e.g. potassium dihydrogen phosphate) |
| Boron | Foliar 0.1% borax or boric acid at flowering and fruit set |
| Calcium | Firms flesh, prevents blossom-end rot |
| Magnesium | Magnesium sulfate mid-to-late season |
| Thinning | 3–4 fruits per cluster (large types), 12–15 (cherry); remove side shoots |

Composted organic, seaweed or amino-acid feeds raise aroma volatiles.

## Verify

```
Ripe-check before picking — all three must be true:
  - Fully in variety colour, no hard green block at the shoulder
  - Gentle press yields slightly and springs back
  - Stem end gives a strong herbal tomato aroma
Storage check: fruit sits stem-down at 20-25°C. Any spot measuring below
  12°C is a fail — chilled fruit goes mealy and does not recover.
Ripening check: hold a colour-break fruit (3-5/10) at room temperature for
  2-3 days; it should colour fully and taste close to vine-ripened.
```

## Facts

- Beyond 10/10 (over-ripe) the flesh goes mealy and the sweet–acid balance drops.
- Organic acids (malic and citric) **decline** during ripening — which is why a
  fruit picked green eventually tastes bland rather than sour: the acid falls but
  the sugar never arrives.
- Aroma volatiles are always lower in off-vine-ripened fruit than vine-ripened,
  regardless of how good the colour looks.
- Nutrition splits by preparation: **raw** preserves vitamin C (15–20 mg/100 g)
  and potassium; **cooked with oil** raises lycopene absorption **2–3×**. Neither
  dominates — they deliver different things.

## Rejected

| Option | Reason |
|---|---|
| Refrigerating to store | Below 12°C destroys aroma-volatile enzymes and the flesh goes mealy permanently. Not recoverable |
| Picking green or early for home eating | Sugar supply is cut at picking; early fruit never regains sweetness or aroma |
| Continuing nitrogen after fruit set | Drives vegetative growth and sour fruit; high-K moves photosynthate into the fruit instead |
| Keeping soil wet through ripening | Dilutes sugars — but cut water gradually, since abrupt swings crack fruit |
| Leaving all fruit on without thinning | Splits limited nutrients across too many fruit; thinning raises both sweetness and individual fruit weight |

## Open

- How hard to water-stress depends on variety and on container vs in-ground
  planting. The 30–50% figure is a starting point, not a universal.
