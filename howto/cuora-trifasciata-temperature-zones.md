# Cuora trifasciata temperature zones

**Scope:** Temperature requirements for the golden coin box turtle
(*Cuora trifasciata*) and the Chinese box turtle (*C. flavomarginata*), and why a
mild-summer maritime climate is unsuitable for full-time outdoor housing.
Excludes diet, breeding and disease.

## Conclusion

**Do not house *C. trifasciata* outdoors full-time in a climate whose summer
nights fall below 68°F (20°C)** — which includes coastal Mediterranean and cool
maritime regions even in July and August, where nights routinely reach the 50s°F.

Required zones during the active season:

| Zone | Target |
|---|---|
| Daytime ambient | **75–85°F** (24–29°C) |
| Basking hotspot | **90–94°F** (32–34°C) |
| Night low | **68–75°F** (20–24°C) |

Indoors, treat 75°F as the *floor*, not the target: add a basking lamp to reach
90–94°F over the land area and let the rest of the enclosure fall to ambient.
A thermal gradient matters more than any single number — the animal
thermoregulates by moving.

## Verify

```
Place two thermometers: one at the basking spot, one at the coolest corner.
  Basking should read 90-94°F; the cool end should not exceed ~80°F.
Read the night low with a min/max thermometer over three consecutive nights
  before trusting a location for full-time outdoor housing.
```

## Facts

- Active-season zones: daytime ambient 75–85°F, basking 90–94°F, night low 68–75°F.
- **Ambient below 70°F slows reptile digestion dramatically.** Damp, cool mornings
  compound it: a cold animal that has eaten cannot process the meal.
- Optimal activity and feeding range for *C. trifasciata*: **25–30°C**. Below 20°C
  metabolism slows markedly.
- Coastal and peninsular maritime sites are the worst case — marine fog holds
  night temperatures down through midsummer.

## Rejected

| Option | Reason |
|---|---|
| Full-time outdoor housing in a cool-summer maritime climate | Night lows in the 50s°F sit far below the 68–75°F requirement, even in midsummer |
| Relying on mean daily temperature | The night low is the binding constraint, and averages hide it |
| A single ambient setpoint with no basking lamp | The animal needs a gradient to thermoregulate; one temperature everywhere gives it no choice |

## Open

- Figures are for the active season. Cooling and brumation protocols vary by
  provenance and are not covered here.
