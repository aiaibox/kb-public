# Never cohouse Cuora species

**Scope:** Why *Cuora flavomarginata* and *C. trifasciata* must be kept in
separate enclosures, and how to diagnose feeding refusal in *C. trifasciata*
kept in an air-conditioned room. Excludes breeding and veterinary treatment.

## Conclusion

**Keep the two species in separate, species-specific enclosures. Never cohabit
them.** Four independent reasons, any one of which is sufficient:

1. **Habitat mismatch.** *C. flavomarginata* is highly terrestrial — damp forest
   floor, burrows into leaf litter, needs only a shallow soak dish, and is a poor
   swimmer that **can drown in deep water**. *C. trifasciata* is strongly
   semi-aquatic and needs substantial deep swimming water plus a basking area.
   Satisfying both means building two habitats inside one enormous tank.
2. **Hybridisation.** Both are genus *Cuora* and can cross-breed. *C. trifasciata*
   is critically endangered in the wild and pure lineages are highly valued;
   intentional hybridisation is strongly discouraged in the conservation and
   breeding community.
3. **Aggression** and competition for food and basking sites.
4. **Cross-species pathogen exposure.**

**For feeding refusal in *C. trifasciata*, suspect temperature before disease.**
In an air-conditioned room the water is almost always the cause:

| Layer | Typical value |
|---|---|
| AC room air | 22–25°C |
| Tank water (evaporative cooling) | **1–2°C below room air** |
| *C. trifasciata* feeding range | **25–30°C** |

So a 23°C room yields ~21–22°C water — at or below the point where digestion
stalls and the animal self-fasts rather than let food ferment in its gut.

The fix, in order of preference:

- **Move the tank** to a room at 26–30°C with no AC draft, or
- **Heat the water in place** with a guarded (anti-burn, plastic-sheathed)
  aquarium heater set to **26–28°C**, water level fully covering the heater's
  thermostat sensor line, tank out of the AC air stream, lid on with a
  ventilation gap.

Expect appetite to return **1–2 days** after the water temperature is stable.

**Escalate to a reptile veterinarian** if there is no improvement after 2–3 days
of warm, quiet rest, or if any symptom appears.

## Verify

```
Tank thermometer reads 26-28°C continuously over 24 h, with no day/night
  swing exceeding 3°C.
Turtle accepts preferred live food (peeled chopped shrimp, small live fish,
  mealworms) within 1-2 days of stable temperature, offered quietly with no
  foot traffic nearby.
Physical check: each turtle is in its own enclosure. No shared tank.
```

## Facts

- Optimal activity and feeding temperature for *C. trifasciata*: **25–30°C**.
  Below 20°C metabolism slows markedly and it reduces or stops eating.
- Tank water sits **1–2°C below room air** because of surface evaporation. This
  is the step most people miss when they set the room thermostat and assume the
  water matches.
- **Water-change stress** occurs if new and old water differ by more than 2°C.
  Keep the day/night swing within 3°C.
- A newly acquired or newly rehoused turtle may need **3–7 days or longer** to
  acclimate before eating normally — do not read that as illness.
- Direct AC draft carries its own risk of respiratory infection, independent of
  water temperature.

## Symptoms that mean stop diagnosing and see a vet

| Condition | Presentation |
|---|---|
| Gastroenteritis | Floating; white or mucous stools; lethargy; frequent basking |
| Pneumonia / respiratory infection | Tilted swimming; open-mouth breathing; clicking respiration; nasal bubbles; eye swelling |
| White-eye disease | Rubbing eyes with forelimbs; red swollen eyes with white discharge; refusing food *because it cannot see it* |

## Rejected

| Option | Reason |
|---|---|
| One enclosure for both species | Terrestrial vs semi-aquatic mismatch, hybridisation risk, aggression, pathogen exposure — four independent disqualifiers |
| Treating feeding refusal as illness first | Temperature is far more common, costs nothing to rule out, and resolves in 1–2 days if it is the cause |
| Setting the room thermostat and assuming the water follows | Water runs 1–2°C cooler than air; the room reading is not the animal's temperature |

## Open

- The escalation thresholds above are field heuristics, not clinical guidance.
  Water quality and new-tank acclimation stress also remain differential
  diagnoses if warming alone fails.
