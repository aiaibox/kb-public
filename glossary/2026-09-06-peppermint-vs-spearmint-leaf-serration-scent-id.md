# Peppermint vs spearmint: leaf, serration, scent ID

**Scope:** Field identification of peppermint (Mentha × piperita) vs spearmint (Mentha spicata) by leaves, leaf edges and scent; does not cover other mint varieties, growing care, or purchasing advice.

## Conclusion

To tell the two mints apart: Peppermint — darker green, broader oval leaves with a relatively smooth surface, stems often purplish; leaf-edge teeth blunt, rounded and evenly spaced; scent intense and cooling, toothpaste-like, because it has higher menthol content. Spearmint — brighter green, longer/narrower leaves with pronounced wrinkled, ridged texture; leaf-edge teeth sharper, deeper and denser, saw-like; scent milder and sweeter (the flavour used in most chewing gums and mojitos). Quick rule: darker leaf + purplish stem + punchy cooling smell = peppermint; light green, elongated, sweet-smelling = spearmint. Confirmed: peppermint is the stronger-smelling of the two.

## Verify

```
Crush a fresh leaf and smell: immediate sharp cooling, toothpaste-like aroma = peppermint; softer, sweet aroma = spearmint.
Inspect the leaf edge: blunt, evenly spaced teeth = peppermint; sharp, deep, dense saw-like teeth = spearmint.
Side-by-side leaf check: broad dark oval leaf, often purplish stem = peppermint; narrow bright green visibly wrinkled leaf = spearmint.
```

## Facts

- Peppermint leaves: darker green, broad/oval, relatively smooth surface; stems sometimes purplish.
- Spearmint leaves: brighter green, elongated, visibly wrinkled or ridged surface.
- Peppermint has higher menthol content than spearmint; its scent is stronger, cooler and toothpaste-like.
- Spearmint scent is milder and sweeter; it is the flavour behind most chewing gums and mojitos.
- Leaf edge: peppermint serration is blunter, rounded and evenly spaced; spearmint serration is sharper, deeper and denser, saw-like.
